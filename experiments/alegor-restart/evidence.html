   8  
   9  The ROMPE trials established the current visual quality bar: **“A memorable overview”** is the only tested output accepted as a natural Represent product direction. The user's desired product is clean and easy at first glance, with deeper summaries, technical data, and source evidence available through hover, focus, or popups. The overview should detect and adopt each project's own style; the MVP narrows the format/workflow to a dependable illustrated overview and cheap, measurable updates, not to a single art style such as ROMPE's pixel art.
  10  
  11  The product should be designed in two equally serious tracks: **Visu**, the illustrated overview that can grow into an animated interactive scene, and **Grafe**, a technical explorer improving on the Codevis idea with source/code detail inside focused nodes. Share project identity, evidence, and snapshot lineage. Implementation need not happen in parallel: the first product proof is an updateable Visu overview; the earlier plane-sync scan becomes a Grafe engineering checkpoint rather than the global gate for all product work. **Alegor** is a candidate name for a project/history companion; its exact product/service boundary remains a proposal, and the user's only fixed constraint is that it is not a mascot.

Names and any `alegor.ai` domain styling are provisional; domain and trademark availability have not been checked. A local-first snapshot workflow is the MVP direction. Hosted sync, embeds, sharing, or subscription budgets are later service proposals, not current commitments.

  17  ## What is Represent
  18  
  19  Represent is the **root workspace for all LLM-based visual representation projects** under waremoto. It lives at `D:/ware/represent` (repo: <https://github.com/Jlabarca/represent>). Multiple concrete projects — different visualization modes, different ingestion strategies, different output surfaces — will live here as sub-projects under one shared doctrine, one AI backend, and one embeddability target.
  20  
  21  Each sub-project ingests source code (files, directory trees, git history, runtime traces) and outputs artistic visual representations — dependency graphs, creatures, cities, blueprints, animated minimaps, whatever makes a codebase legible at a glance.
  22  
  23  The thesis: a project should have an iconic visual identity that reflects its real structure, not just a logo. Developers should look at the picture and understand relationships they'd otherwise have to read thousands of lines to recover.
