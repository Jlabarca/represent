49 ## 1. Pillars
50 
51 1. **Show, don't tell.** Two lines maximum, everywhere: dialogue, notifications, tooltips. If it
52    needs three lines, it needs a picture, a glow, an arrow or a demo instead. A Kafra line looks
53    like *"Quest completed!"*, not a paragraph.
54 2. **Fun first, fast.** The first 15 minutes show the best of the game. The first two hours put
55    every core loop in your hands once. Systems explain themselves by being used.
56 3. **Organic, never errands-for-errands.** The world offers. You never walk to an NPC to be told
57    to walk to another NPC.
58 4. **Hints of the endgame early.** You see caravans, Graves, crafted knights and Rifts in the first
59    minutes, and you borrow a taste of endgame power once (§4.2).
60 5. **Risk is a place, never a surprise.** Colour-coded zones, a border banner, a carried-value
61    meter.
62 6. **Every drop has a buyer, every death a demand.** The Albion economy runs on RO's own loot.
63 7. **Built the ORO way.** Every mechanic is one of: a rule layer (ambient map mode), an event layer,
64    a quest-platform entry, or (only when nothing else works) a core system. Turn a layer off and
65    the map is vanilla again.
66 8. **Vanilla at default dials.** The Sandbox adds rules, items, places and services; it never
67    retunes a pinned formula. World Dials at default stay bitwise vanilla.
68 9. **Smooth and cheap.** The CLAUDE.md UI bar applies to every surface here: a Ledger or Kafra
69    card that stutters is a bug.
70 10. **Tunable live.** Every number in this file is a dial with a GhostShell Break view (§17).

74 ## 2. The world, lightly
75 
76 - **Rune-Midgard is a digital world**, and **Kafra Corp** is what keeps it from falling apart:
77   part Capsule Corp (the technology: warps, storage, the Link device, the market grid), part
78   Pokémon Center (healing, saving, safety) and part Officer Jenny (order, bounties, the zone law).
79 - **The problem:** monster invasions are getting more common, and nobody knows why. Cities need
80   defending (the Mob War), roads need guarding (Caravans), and something is rotting the wild
81   places (Rifts, black zones). Players find out why, season by season.
82 - **Lore stays light.** It's told through events and two-line Kafra pings, never codex pages.
83   The why-mystery is the seasonal spine; [rompe-server-lore.md](../research/rompe-server-lore.md)
84   and the Mob War story (Geffen, the Tower, the Orcs, Aldebaran) slot under it.
85 - **Kafra Corp owns the services** in this design: the Link, the Ledger, the market board,
86   warehouses, Caravans, Kafra Hire (characters), Graves' registry, the zone law. One brand for
87   "how the world works" keeps the UI and the lore simple.

91 ## 3. The Kafra Link: the navi
92 
93 ### 3.1 What it is
94 
95 - A **communication device** every character carries. Through it a Kafra talks to you from
96   anywhere, like a navi in a Zelda or Battle Network game. Inside the world it's Kafra Corp's
97   support line.
98 - **It is the tutorial, hidden.** Nothing is labelled "tutorial". The Link notices what you're
99   doing and says one line at the right moment.
100 - **It's the quest tracker's voice.** Errand progress, completions, prize delivery, layer offers.
101 - **It automates the boring parts:** prizes are delivered automatically (inventory, or the
102   city warehouse when you're full); finished Wants ping "Ready to craft"; "Quest completed!" is
103   the whole message.
104 
105 ### 3.2 How it speaks
106 
107 | Rule | Value |
108 |---|---|
109 | Max length | 2 lines, about 60 characters each |
110 | Voice | Short, warm, a little cheeky. Never explains a system in words |
111 | Channel | A small Kafra portrait + line at a screen edge, 3 s, then it folds into the history |
112 | Rate | Max one line every ~20 s unless you're acting on it; an important line can wait for calm |
113 | Pointing | A line can carry a **show**: a glow on a monster, an arrow to a spot, a window that opens on click |
114 
115 ### 3.3 Off, focus and history
116 
117 - **Off from the start:** a single switch on the first screen ("Kafra Link: On / Off") and in
118   Settings. Off means silence forever; the history still records.
119 - **Focus mode** (a hotkey and an HUD toggle): the Link queues lines silently during a fight, a
120   minigame or a layer, then shows one summary chip ("3 updates") afterwards.
121 - **History:** a **Kafra tab in the ESC menu.** Every line, newest first, grouped by session;
122   each line keeps its deep link (open the Want, the map spot, the window). It's where "what happened
123   while I was in focus mode" lives.
124 
125 ### 3.4 Built from what exists
126 
127 - The **notification contract** exists: `ORO.Contracts.Notifications.Notification`
128   (id-replaces-in-place, `Kind`, `Title`, `Body`, `DeepLink`), the deep-link registry
129   (`NotificationDeepLinkRegistry`), `IModNotificationService`, the `notifications` mod channel and
130   `UI/Windows/Notifications/NotificationHistory.cs`. **The tray (NTF.2) isn't built.** The Link is
131   that tray with a Kafra face, a rate limiter and a focus mode.
132 - The ESC menu exists (`UI/Windows/EscMenuWindow.cs`); the Kafra tab is a new Maqui window
133   launched from it.
134 - The guide Kafra already greets new characters (`Script/Npcs/Kafra.txt`, `@kafra.welcome`) and
135   offers the Super Novice skip (CR.9.1). The Link replaces the walk-to-talk with a call.

139 ## 4. The first 15 minutes and the first two hours
140 
141 ### 4.1 Principles
142 
143 - You arrive **outside** the starter city, in a living field, not in a menu or an NPC queue.
144 - Every lesson is an action with an immediate reward. The Link says one line; the world does the
145   rest.
146 - **No quest givers in the first 15 minutes.** The Link is the only voice.
147 
148 ### 4.2 The first 15 minutes (a sketch to playtest, not a script to ship)
149 
150 | Minute | What happens | What it teaches | Show |
151 |---|---|---|---|
152 | 0 | You land in a field outside the city. Porings bounce around. One glows: a **Poporing with a knife inside it**. Link: *"A Poporing ate a knife! Get it back?"* | Movement, targeting | the glow |
153 | 1–3 | You fight Porings to reach it. Each kill pops loot and a little EXP | Basic attack, loot pickup | damage numbers, loot pop |
154 | 3 | A Poring hits you hard. Your **First Aid** (Novices start with it) heals **×3** here. Link: *"Ouch. Try First Aid."* | Your first skill, the hotbar | a pulse on the hotbar slot |
155 | 4 | The Poporing pops; a **Stiletto** drops. One tap equips it. Link: *"Nice blade!"* | Equipment | auto-equip prompt |
156 | 5 | You pick up your 5th Jellopy. Link: *"Jellopy! A few more = a better knife."* The Ledger opens its first **Want** with a counter | Wants, counters | the Ledger card slides in |
157 | 6–8 | A **caravan** rolls along the road, escorted by high-level players and bots. Somewhere, a black-zone **Grave** blinks into the kill feed | The world beyond | you see it; nobody explains it |
158 | 8 | **A taste of the endgame:** a Thief Bug swarm. Kafra Hire lends you a **rented level-80 companion** for 2 minutes (§9.5). It wrecks the swarm. Link: *"That's an account. You'll have one."* The rental ends with a dissolve | The company, power, the future | the borrowed power |
159 | 10 | Want complete. Link: *"Ready to craft!"* A one-tap craft at the city gate's Kafra workshop gives you a crafted T1 knife | Crafting, the station | a forge spark |
160 | 12 | Your 10th job level arrives. Link: *"Job Master's free."* (Classic Return: no quest.) | First job | an arrow |
161 | 15 | You're a first-job character with a crafted knife, a Want running and a glimpse of everything | — | — |
162 
163 Notes:
164 - **First Aid ×3** is a starter-zone dial (`starter.firstAidMultiplier = 3`), not a formula change,
165   and it ends at the city's outer fields. It's a quiet hint that the game wants you to fight, not
166   rest.
167 - The Poporing and the swarm are **layer-owned spawns** of a "starter field" map mode, so they
168   never pollute the vanilla spawn tables (cleanup promise).
169 - The rented companion in minute 8 is the real Kafra Hire feature in miniature: the onboarding
170   sells D16 without words.
171 
172 ### 4.3 The first two hours
173 
174 Each step is offered by the Link when you're near it, never forced, never a fetch chain:
175 
176 | Step | Loop introduced | Trigger |
177 |---|---|---|
178 | A second Want, from a monster drop | Material Atlas answers ("Wolf · pay_fild08") | picking up a material |
179 | First job | Classic Return | job level 10 |
180 | **Your account of two** | the roster: call a second character | base level ~15; Link: *"Want a partner?"* |
181 | A yellow field | flags, the risk HUD | walking past the first yellow border |
182 | A Surge | gathering | a surge spawns on a map near you |
183 | **Your first Rift** | instanced 1v1, gear-capped | a Rift opens on your map |
184 | A caravan escort | layers, pay, sides | a caravan leaves your city |
185 | Your first commission or board sale | the market | your first unused crafted item |
186 
187 After two hours a player has touched every loop once. The **mid-game** (base 30–70) is about
188 deepening them: a crafter in your account, a guild, red zones, your first outpost fight.

192 ## 5. The quest platform, completed: Places, Errands, Opportunities, the Ledger
193 
194 ### 5.1 Today
195 
196 - **There:** minigame places (`QuestPlace`, `PlaceKind.Arcade`, store, giver walk-to, leaderboards;
197   Frontris only); skill-gather opportunity offers (`OpportunityService`, contribution credit).
198 - **Missing:** any tracked objective, any quest log, any tracker UI. The opportunity offer's
199   progress never reaches the client (`SkillGatherClientState.cs`).
200 
201 ### 5.2 Three kinds, one Ledger
202 
203 | Kind | What | Example | Status |
204 |---|---|---|---|
205 | **Place** | a minigame you enter and score | Frontris; the forge minigame | built (Arcade) |
206 | **Errand** | a tracked goal in the shared world | a Want; "escort the Geffen caravan"; "close 3 Rifts" | **new** |
207 | **Opportunity** | the world offers you something because of where you are | "learn Sonic Blow 0/10"; "an Iron vein surges nearby"; "a Rift opened" | built (server) |
208 
209 - **The Ledger** is the single tracker: a HUD card (standard event-layer surface) with pinned
210   entries and live counters, plus a full window. The server is authoritative on a `ledger` mod
211   channel.
212 - **The Link is the Ledger's voice.** Progress and completions are Link lines (§3); clicking one
213   opens the Ledger entry.
214 
215 ### 5.3 Errand verbs (closed list)
216 
217 | Verb | Progress from | Example |
218 |---|---|---|
219 | `have(item, n)` | inventory + cart + warehouse/storage, read live | every Want |
220 | `defeat(monster set, area, n)` | contribution credit (never last hit) | "clear 30 Orcs at the outpost" |
221 | `reach(map[, cell])` | arrival | "find the Road entrance" |
222 | `take-part(layer, outcome)` | layer membership + its result event | "escort a caravan to arrival" |
223 | `deliver(item, n, target)` | handover to an NPC, a warehouse or a caravan | "bring 50 Iron to Prontera's forge" |
224 
225 - **Five verbs, closed.** Anything else is a Place (a minigame) or a layer. That amends the
226   playbook's "never grow an objective vocabulary" (§18).
227 - Errands are **data** (TOML/JSON), never NPC scripts with flags. Givers, if any, are platform-spawned.
228 - **Errands can pay** items, zeny and EXP (§15). The cosmetic-only rule stays on minigame *scores*.
229 - **Prizes are delivered automatically** (D18): no "return to A".
230 
231 ### 5.4 Opportunities: the world talks
232 
233 - **Material:** killing a monster that drops a Want material, or standing near a Surge, makes the
234   Ledger nudge: *"Wolves drop Wolf Claw. 4/15."*
235 - **Layer:** a caravan leaving town, a Rift opening, your guild's outpost under attack. Offered,
236   never forced (`DefaultEntry=Offered`).
237 - **Skill scrolls** (Classic Return's rare prize) and skill-gather offers for Ultra Novices ride the
238   same channel.

242 ## 6. Crafting for people who hate crafting
243 
244 ### 6.1 The Crafting Book
245 
246 - One window lists **every recipe**, unlocked for every character from level 1. You don't learn
247   recipes; you learn *where things come from*.
248 - Filters: slot, who can wear it, tier, "I can make it now", "I have ≥ 50%".
249 - Each card: materials, who can craft it (§6.4), market prices of the result and of each material.
250 
251 ### 6.2 Want it
252 
253 Press **Want** on a recipe and it becomes an Errand in your Ledger:
254 
255 ```
256 WANT  Crafted Mace T3                        [Craft now ▸]
257   Iron            12 / 20   ▸ Orc Warrior · gef_fild14 · lv 24 · yellow
258   Steel            1 / 3    ▸ board: 1,214z
259   Jellopy        210 / 200 ✓
260   Wolf Claw        4 / 15   ▸ Wolf · pay_fild08 · lv 18 · blue
261                             ▸ Surge: Howling Hills, in 6 min
262 ```
263 
264 - **Counters are what you have**, read live. "Collect N" needs no kill counter.
265 - **Every `▸` is a Material Atlas answer:** the nearest map at your level, in your risk colour; a
266   live Surge; the board price. Click to auto-path or to buy.
267 - Several Wants stack, and shared materials are counted once.
268 - When every row is green, the Link says *"Ready to craft!"*
269 
270 ### 6.3 The Material Atlas: one data product, two users
271 
272 - Generated offline from `Db/` drops + spawn files + map tags + risk colours:
273   `material → [(monster, map, band, colour, rate, expected kills per unit)]`,
274   `material → [surge kinds]`, `material → [recipes]`.
275 - **The game** uses it for the `▸` answers. **The tooling** uses it for recipe coverage per band and
276   the economy-flow views (§16, §17). Built like `bin/player-path-mapfacts.py`; shipped as data.
277 
278 ### 6.4 Three doors to a craft
279 
280 | Door | Who | Quality | Material return | Cost |
281 |---|---|---|---|---|
282 | **Kafra workshop** | anyone, in any city | Normal only | 0% | a fee (zeny sink) |
283 | **Your account's crafter** | a Blacksmith/Alchemist in your roster | up to Masterpiece | by skills + city bonus | Focus (§10.3) |
284 | **Commission** | post the Want on the board; any player crafter fills it | theirs | theirs | your offer |
285 
286 - A crafting-hater never touches an anvil, but still feeds the economy by hunting.
287 - A crafting-lover owns the high end: only player crafters reach Good/Excellent/Masterpiece, and
288   commissions bring customers.
289 
290 ### 6.5 The forge minigame (optional)
291 
292 - **Craft now** with your own crafter offers **Quick** (instant, Normal-to-Good roll) or **Forge**
293   (a 20–40 s minigame, `PlaceKind.Arcade` like Frontris). Smith: a hammer-timing heat bar.
294   Alchemist: a mixing bar.
295 - The score → quality roll → a weekly leaderboard per city. Plausibility bounds as on the
296   platform; quality caps stay modest because it isn't anti-cheat.
297 
298 ### 6.6 RO's etc. items are the materials
299 
300 - Hundreds of etc. drops (Jellopy, Feather, Wolf Claw, Tooth of Bat…) only sell to NPCs today.
301   **Crafted recipes consume them.** Grinding Wolves means something again.
302 - Ores and crafting items already exist: Iron 998, Steel 999, Star Crumb 1000, Iron Ore 1002,
303   Coal 1003, elemental stones 994–997, Anvil 986, Mini Furnace 612.
304 - New materials only for tiers and zones RO never had (T6+ ores from black zones).
305 
306 ### 6.7 Salvage
307 
308 Any crafted or looted gear salvages at a workshop into a share of its materials (start 25%).
309 PvP loot feeds crafting; storage stops filling with junk.

313 ## 7. The crafted tier
314 
315 - **T1–T8**, crafted only. T3 ≈ an average first-job drop, T6 ≈ an average second-job drop,
316   T8 ≈ endgame-competitive. Vanilla items stay, unchanged.
317 - **Quality** per craft: Normal / Good / Excellent / Masterpiece.
318 - **Sockets: at least 1 on every crafted weapon**, more by tier (T1–T3: 1, T4–T6: 2, T7–T8: 3).
319   Armour by tier. Today `PacketSocketEquipment` refuses crafted items; that changes.
320 - **Durability** on crafted items only, worn by deaths in yellow and above; repaired at a workshop
321   or by a Blacksmith (Weapon Repair, enabled).
322 - **The maker's mark**: "Made by X", shown in the tooltip and on the kill feed.
323 - **Elements**: Enchanted Stone Craft makes elemental crafted weapons (classic forging's
324   signature). Item-borne *skills*: not now (§20).
325 
326 ### 7.1 The data change to make first
327 
328 - Gear is a 40-byte `UniqueItem`; its four `Data` ints are the card slots
329   (`RebuildSharedData/Data/ItemSlotEntry.cs`). Tier, quality, durability and the maker's mark need
330   room.
331 - Recommended: a versioned **`CraftInfo` block** (tier, quality, durability, crafter character id),
332   gated by the existing `CraftedItem` flag. It touches the shared `.dll` (`updateclient.bat`), the
333   DB blob and the wire: its own phase, with a migration test that *runs* the migration.

337 ## 8. Cards: common sockets, two faces
338 
339 ### 8.1 Sockets everywhere (D15)
340 
341 - **Every weapon has at least one socket**: crafted ones by tier (§7), and **vanilla weapons with
342   0 slots gain 1**. That's a change to pinned slot counts, listed in §18.
343 - Armour keeps its pinned slots for now; it's revisited in the card overhaul.
344 - Classic RO's card system (one card, one effect, compounded into a slot) carries over as it is.
345 
346 ### 8.2 One card, two faces (direction; its own design later)
347 
348 The operator expects to overhaul cards and to meet Spawn Cards. The shape that fits both:
349 
350 - **A card is one item with two faces.** The **Socket face** is the classic effect when compounded
351   into gear. The **Summon face** is its Spawn Cards use in a deck (Binder, Muster, duels).
352 - **You choose per copy:** compound it (it leaves the Binder while socketed) or deck it. A card
353   can't be both at once. Card removal (`CardRemover`, already built) returns it to the Binder.
354 - **Not every card needs both faces.** Some are socket-only, some summon-only. The Card Forge
355   (`card-forge.md`) is where faces are authored.
356 - This is a **direction, not a design.** It needs its own doc, reconciled with
357   [spawn-cards-layer.md](spawn-cards-layer.md), [card-album.md](card-album.md) and
358   [card-forge.md](card-forge.md). The Spawn Cards rule "cards are unlocked, never crafted" is about
359   the summon face and stays true.

363 ## 9. Your account: a roster of 12 (core system)
364 
365 ### 9.1 The name: account (decided)
366 
367 - **The roster is your account** (operator, 2026-10-20). It isn't a separate group with its own
368   name: it's a **tag on party members that are yours** ("I called my account!").
369 - All grouping (account, hires, party, guild, layer sides) is designed in one place:
370   **[groups.md](groups.md)**. That doc also lists the factions/PvP fixes the Sandbox needs.
371 
372 ### 9.2 What it is
373 
374 - An account holds **up to 12 characters** (today: 3; `PacketEnterServer.cs` rejects `slot > 2`,
375   `CharacterSelectWindow.cs` loops 3).
376 - Each keeps **classic job rules** (Novice → first → second job).
377 - One is the **main** (your connection drives it). Others **rest** (offline) or come along as
378   **companions** (in the world, following).
379 - A companion is the **real character**: same inventory, levels, death.
380 
381 ### 9.3 How it's built
382 
383 | Need | Built from | Status |
384 |---|---|---|
385 | A character in the world with no connection | bot `Player`s (`World.CreateBotPlayer`, `IsBot`) | built |
386 | Following and stances | `FollowHumanAction`, `BotBrain` `HoldStance` | built |
387 | The main commanding companions | `PacketPlayerBatchCommand` + `CommandAuthorization` owner lease (`CommandGrants.Grant`) | built (Spawn Cards uses it) |
388 | Swapping which body you drive | `ImpersonationService` re-binds a connection (admin-only today) | **generalise** |
389 | EXP sharing | classic party even-share, pinned | built |
390 | Loading a resting character as an owned body | "call from roster" | **new** |
391 
392 The bot model holds: **one owner per body.** The Roster adds **the account** as an owner kind; the
393 Overmind never drafts a companion.
394 
395 ### 9.4 Companions are cards
396 
397 - The Binder gets an **Account page**: each character as a card (job sprite, level, gear power).
398 - **Calling a companion is casting its card**: card on the floor, pixel arrival. Dismiss = back to
399   rest.
400 - **The pet side of the Spawn Cards HUD shows your companions** next to summons: HP/SP, stance,
401   "swap to".
402 - **Excluded from duels, the Muster and instanced PvP** (cap 0); Standard Rules stay equal.
403 
404 ### 9.5 Kafra Hire: trade, adopt, hire, rent (D16)
405 
406 The point (operator): **you don't always have to level a character from 1.**
407 
408 | Action | What | Rules |
409 |---|---|---|
410 | **Trade** | sell or give one of your characters to another account | zeny only; a fee; 7-day lock before it can be traded again; the seller chooses "with gear" or "bare" (bare moves its gear to the seller's storage first); guild and party memberships end |
411 | **Adopt** | buy a **population bot's character** | the bot leaves the population (Vivid supplies a replacement); priced by level and gear; rename allowed once |
412 | **Hire** | a population bot joins your party for an hour | a `hireling` task-board demand, `OwnerMoves`; paid per hour (sink); fights and buffs, **never crafts** |
413 | **Rent** | another player's (or a bot's) character joins your party for a while | §9.6, the hard one |
414 
415 ### 9.6 Renting characters without coming back to a mess
416 
417 The fear: *"you relogin the next day to find your char in a mess, or missing."* The answer is a
418 **Lease**, the same idea ORO already uses for overrides (a scoped, expiring, server-held grant).
419 
420 **While a character is rented:**
421 
422 | Locked (renter can't) | Allowed (renter can) |
423 |---|---|
424 | Unequip, drop, trade, vend, store, refine, socket or salvage any equipped item | Fight, use skills, move, party |
425 | Open the character's own inventory, cart or zeny | Use a **rental bag** of the renter's own consumables, which goes back to the renter |
426 | Spend stat or skill points | Earn EXP (the owner chooses: the character keeps it, or it's converted to zeny for the owner) |

432 - The renter pays the rent plus a **deposit**, held by Kafra Corp.
433 - Durability wear and repair are taken from the deposit.
434 - If the owner allows red or black, the **deposit must cover the equipped gear's value**: a Grave
435   drop is paid to the owner from the deposit. Black zones (crafted country) already keep vanilla

145 ### 4.1 Big parties: data at scale, without opening the door to abuse
146 
147 The operator's question: *how do MMOs offer that much data to every client without getting DDoSed
148 or hacked?* The answer is a handful of rules, and the party panel follows all of them:
149 
150 | Rule | What it means here |
151 |---|---|
152 | **Interest management** | A client only receives live data for what's relevant: party members **near you** get live HP/SP; far ones get a slow summary (map, online, level) every few seconds. The server already does this for entities (chunk-bucketed visibility) |
153 | **Pull on demand, push only deltas** | The panel shows the top rows live (tagged members first) and fetches the rest **as you scroll** (a virtualised list, paged by the server). Changes go out as small deltas, never the full roster |
154 | **Aggregates over rows** | For a 1,000-member party or guild, the header shows counts ("812 online, 40 near you") from a server aggregate, not 1,000 rows |
155 | **Server-authoritative, minimal fields** | Clients never get what they don't need: no positions of far members, no inventories, no IPs, no account ids. A member row is a public card |
156 | **Budgets** | Every client request type has a rate budget per connection and per account; over budget means dropped and logged. Big reads (a full guild roster) are cached server-side for a few seconds and shared by everyone who asks |
157 | **Public data via cache, not the game socket** | Killboards, guild rosters and rankings anyone may read go out over HTTP behind the CDN (Cloudflare already fronts break.maremoto.dev), so a flood hits the cache, not the game server |
158 | **Auth on every private read** | The session token gates private views (your party, your guild); the server checks membership on each request |
159 
160 Client side, the UI bar applies: a virtualised list, no per-frame allocations, only on-screen rows
161 built. Tagged members (account, hire) always render as full rows at the top.

15 ## 0. The honest read on the niche
16 
17 - **Classic-RO nostalgia is a real market but a shrinking one.** Private servers prove it lives;
18   they also prove it churns. People return for a fresh server, play hard for 2–6 months, and
19   leave once the economy settles.
20 - **Albion shows the other half: a top-down, click-to-move MMO with *very* simple controls can be
21   a durable hit.** It looks enough like RO that its controls are not the gap. What made it last is
22   **risk, a player economy, short sessions and one cross-platform world.**
23 - **"Modern RO in retro pixel art" isn't a big audience on its own.** Albion-grade PvP plus
24   the RO world *is*: it's the only top-down PvP sandbox with RO's monsters, cards and jobs.
25   So the pitch is "Albion's loop running on RO's content", not "RO with better graphics".
26 - **Charter compatibility.** None of this needs the pre-RE formulas touched. Albion-style systems
27   are **layers and map modes on top of the vanilla world** (risk zones, rotating roads, invasion
28   dungeons, faction territory), which is exactly what the mod and map-mode platform is for.
29   Vanilla zones stay vanilla, and the risk lives in its own places.
30 - **Albion has loading screens.** Its world is ~hundreds of small zones joined by exits. It
31   *feels* big because **moving between zones costs you risk and time**, not because the zones are
32   seamless. That changes what "fusing maps" should mean for us (see AL-11 and X-1).

118 ## 3. Why each grade
119 
120 ### Albion
121 
122 **AL-1 Risk-coloured zones (STEAL).** This is Albion's spine and the main reason it has lasted.
123 Every trip is a choice of how much to risk for how much reward, and that one choice drives
124 PvP, the economy and the drama. **ORO cost B:** map tags exist ([map-tags.md](../reference/map-tags.md)),
125 `DeathPenalty` exists, the faction system already flags players and map modes are mods. A
126 `risk=blue|yellow|red|black` tag plus a per-tier death rule is a layer, not a rewrite. **Value A:**
127 it turns RO's existing field maps into PvP content with no new art.
128 
129 **AL-2 Full loot (ADAPT).** It's the identity of Albion's black zones, and also what scares
130 off half of its potential players (Praise B). In RO it is more dangerous: a 4-card weapon at +9 is
131 months of work, and losing it all at once makes people quit. Adapt it as **tiers of loss**: equipped
132 cards are never lootable; black zones drop the inventory plus one random unequipped item;
133 "frontier gear" (AL-4) drops in full. **ORO cost C:** a death container entity, loot rules, and
134 logging so GMs can settle disputes.
135 
136 **AL-3 Gear destruction (ADAPT, depends on AL-4).** Destroying gear is what keeps Albion's crafters
137 employed. Without a crafted supply, though, destruction only deletes drops, and RO's drop economy
138 can't refill them. Do it only on the crafted frontier tier.
139 
140 **AL-4 Player-crafted economy (ADAPT-lite).** It's Albion's most praised and most imitated
141 system (Praise A). It is also **E for us**: RO supplies items through mob drops pinned to rAthena,
142 and replacing that breaks the charter and every drop table. The lite version: a **parallel
143 "frontier" item tier** (siege gear, PvP consumables, zone-specific armour) that is only crafted,
144 from materials gathered in risk zones, and is meant to be lost. Vanilla RO items stay as they are.
145 Forge and refine code already exists to build on.
146 
147 **AL-5 Tiered gathering (LATER).** Gathering only matters once AL-4-lite exists. On its own it's
148 a big content-authoring job (nodes, tiers, respawns, tools).
149 
150 **AL-6 Regional markets (ADAPT).** Not having a global auction house is why hauling goods is a
151 gameplay loop in Albion: transporters, gankers, escorts. RO already has vending and weight limits,
152 and has no auction house. Add a **per-city market board** (buy and sell orders, local to the city)
153 and let risk zones sit between cities. Cost C; it reuses the vending backend.
154 
155 **AL-7 Black Market (LATER).** It's an elegant sink that links crafters to PvE loot, but it only
156 makes sense once a crafted tier exists.
157 
158 **AL-8 Gold/premium exchange (Frontera only).** It works: whales fund the game and players
159 get premium through play. On a RO-IP server, though, **any monetization is the legal tripwire**
160 ([legal-risk.md](../design/legal-risk.md)). Design it for the Frontera profile only.
161 
162 **AL-9 Classless "wear your class" (SKIP).** It's loved in Albion, but the 2026-10-12 Classic Return
163 charter makes classic jobs the trunk. RO's jobs, cards and builds already deliver build variety
164 that Albion players envy. The part worth keeping is AL-28 (build sharing).
165 
166 **AL-10 Destiny board (SKIP).** Albion's own players complain about the mastery grind. RO
167 already has job levels.
168 
169 **AL-11 Roads of Avalon (STEAL). This is the "big maps" answer.** It's a hidden network of
170 small zones whose links reshuffle every few hours. Guilds scout routes, carve out shortcuts and
171 ambush each other. Praise A: the roads are the most talked-about content Albion has added.
172 **ORO cost B:** we already have **dozens of maps nobody visits**: 65 parked post-frontier maps,
173 plus the in-era maps that see no traffic, plus Mapforge to remix any of them at runtime (walls,
174 spawns, sky). A rotating warp graph over those maps is mostly server logic: a graph, a rotation
175 timer, portal NPCs, and a "road" map tag that turns on AL-1 rules. That gives a world that feels
176 huge and always new without authoring a single map. Parked maps would be used as **layout
177 only**, with no out-of-era monsters spawned, so the charter holds.
178 
179 **AL-12 The Mists (STEAL).** Solo or duo entry into a small instanced PvPvE zone, with
180 light loot stakes. It fixed Albion's "I play alone and got farmed by a zerg" problem. **ORO
181 cost C:** instances, queues and map modes exist; what's missing is a matchmaker that puts
182 players of similar gear into the same instance, and the zone rules.
183 
184 **AL-13 Corrupted Dungeons (STEAL FIRST).** You run a solo PvE dungeon, and another player can
185 invade to hunt you. Short (10–15 min), tense, and the best **"my first PvP"** in the genre
186 (Praise A). **ORO cost B:** this is nearly what the [invasion layer](../design/invasion-layer.md)
187 already does: an instanced map, monster packs from the spawn tables, a player injected as the
188 hostile, a loot pot. Of the high-value rows, this is the cheapest. Classic RO dungeons
189 (Glast Heim, Clock Tower, Pyramids) are the obvious skins.
190 
191 **AL-14 Hellgates (ADAPT).** 2v2 and 5v5 fights with loot at stake are liked, but the population
192 needed to fill them is real. Keep the format and let bots fill missing seats.
193 
194 **AL-15 Ranked arena (LATER).** Even Albion's queues struggle. A small server's ranked queue
195 dies first. Ship it after there's a population to fill it.
196 
197 **AL-16 Territory warfare (ADAPT).** Albion's ZvZ is legendary but burns out its elite guilds
198 (Praise B). RO already has **War of Emperium**, and the code has Emperium and guilds. The adaptation

200 scheduled windows, a tax on gathering or the market inside them). It reuses the code and the
201 players' memory of WoE.
202

204 should **measure our own cap first** (server tick + WebGL client FPS with 100+ visible entities)
205 before designing content that depends on huge battles.

207 **AL-18 Faction Warfare (STEAL).** Any player can flag for a city faction, join open-world
208 skirmishes and run "hearts" (an escort objective) between bases. It gives solo and casual

212 **AL-19 Bandit Assault (STEAL).** A timed, server-wide event where factions fight over

219 **AL-21 Laborers (SKIP).** Players find them a chore. Our bots already cover "something

289 first week, mostly over PC performance and missing UI. It's the same bar as CLAUDE.md's UI rule:
290 polish and frame rate are features, and a launch is judged on them.
291 
292 **MM-1 Auto-battle (ADAPT, capped).** Mobile RO (Ragnarok M/Origin) players expect it; PC
293 players see it as botting. ORO already has bots and an AFK substrate. Offer **capped offline
294 training** (a daily budget, safe zones only, never in risk zones).
295 
296 **MM-2 Activity hub (STEAL).** One window: today's events, MVP timers, queue buttons, faction
297 status, season progress. It answers the first question every returning player asks. It's a
298 Maqui window over data that already exists.
299 
300 **MM-3 Queue from anywhere (STEAL).** Queues exist (carnival / party layers). Make every
301 instanced activity queueable from the hub.
302 
303 **MM-4 Fresh / seasonal servers (STEAL).** RO private servers live off fresh launches, and OSRS
304 Leagues show seasonal worlds working at scale. Our **profiles** system is already the
305 machinery: a seasonal profile with its own dials, rule set and end date.

307 ### Others
308 
309 **OT-1 Player polls (STEAL).** OSRS's polls, where content needs 70% to pass, are why a
310 retro-niche community trusts its developer. Free to run on Discord or in-game, and they fit a
311 small community perfectly.
312 
313 **OT-2 Leagues / Deadman (STEAL).** These are seasonal rule-set worlds (relics, boosted rates,
314 full-loot Deadman). They're the strongest proof that a retro MMO can run seasonal modes on top of
315 an unchanged base game, which is our profiles + map modes + World Dials model.
316 
317 **OT-3 New World (guardrail).** It launched with open-world PvP, retreated to opt-in, and shed
318 players both times. **Keep the PvP in clearly marked places (AL-1) from day one**, and never
319 flip a safe area to PvP after the fact.
320 
321 **OT-4 RNG destruction (guardrail).** Refine breaking is pinned pre-RE and stays. Risk zones
322 must not *also* take a refined item away by surprise: AL-2's loss tiers protect it.
323 
324 **X-1 True map fusion (SKIP → edge stitching).** Fusing RO maps into seamless regions means a
325 client that streams terrain, a server that hands entities across map borders, and art seams on
326 every edge. **D/D for a C payoff**, since Albion proves seamlessness isn't what makes a world
327 feel big. The cheap 80%: **edge stitching**. Walking off a field's edge warps you to the
328 neighbour, with the neighbour preloaded in the background so there's no loading screen (the
329 asset system already preloads bundles). Combined with AL-11, the world feels continuous and huge.

335 A coherent ROMPE PvP loop, ordered by value ÷ ORO cost:

337 1. **AL-23 Killboard + kill feed**: days of work, and it starts the community talking.

342 5. **AL-11 Roads of Avalon** from parked and unvisited maps, plus **X-1 edge stitching**: the
343    "huge world".
344 6. **MM-2 Activity hub + MM-3 queues + HK-6 ergonomics**, then **AL-26 mobile/touch**.

351 are there, and what's missing is assembling them into a **risk → reward → loss → rebuild** loop.
